# The Path of Angels terminal speaks one language

*Written 2026-08-09, after a copy pass over `poa-web/src/**`, `poa-web/index.html`
and the PoA strings in `extension/src/**`.*

The fiction on this site is good and the machinery talked like a machine. This is
the note that keeps the next writer from re-litigating the difference. It is short
on purpose: five rules, one glossary, one before/after table.

---

## Why this is hard, and what it is not permission to do

Every string this pass touched was **already true**. They read like a build log
because they were written by people who refused to say anything they had not
established — and that refusal is the most valuable thing on the page. Two days
before this pass, the live beta could not decode counter 10 and every card on the
rack said *AWAITING CURATOR ACTIVATION — "the signed catalog does not enrol it at
this counter."* The catalog enrolled all seven. The terminal had put the curator's
name on its own decoding failure, in a sentence written to sound reassuring.

So: **this is a translation, not a softening.**

- ⚠ **Precision is not the thing being traded away.** "Something went wrong" is
  worse than the jargon, not better. The target is *precise AND human*: the same
  true sentence, in the world's own register.
- ⚠ **Never invent a state.** No line may imply the terminal knows something it
  does not. If two states differ, they get two sentences, however similar they
  look on screen.
- ⚠ **Say who decided.** These are three different facts and they stay three:
  1. **a curator decision** — "the curator has not opened this";
  2. **our defect** — "this terminal could not read the manifest";
  3. **not yet known** — "still reading the manifest".
  Collapsing any two of them is the bug this whole vocabulary exists to prevent.
- ⚠ **A refusal code is never softened away.** Codes are searchable, quotable in a
  bug report, and matchable against the node. Every place a code was rendered, it
  still is — what changed is that a sentence in front of it says what it *means*.
- **Keep the register.** This is a salvage station on a ship, not a SaaS dashboard.
  The emitted game blurbs are the voice to match:

  > *Nine breaths of air, three passages you cannot see, and a climb back out.*
  > *One law hides in a sixteen-law manual. Four charges to find it, then name it.*
  > *Ask a sealed unit yes or no until its shape has nowhere left to hide.*
  > *Every rung down pays more and floods more. The odds are on the button.*

## The glossary

Internal identifiers — state names in code, refusal codes, wire field names, Lean
module paths — **do not change**. This table is only about what a player reads.

| machinery | the ship says |
| --- | --- |
| the signed catalog · the content bundle · POAG1 | **the curator's manifest**, or just **the manifest** |
| content epoch *N*, counter *M* | **manifest revision *N.M*** |
| authenticate / authentication | **check** · **the signature checks out** · **did not check out** |
| enrolled at this counter | **on the manifest** · **the curator has opened it** |
| not enrolled | **the curator has not opened this** |
| controller installed / no controller | **this terminal can run it** / **cannot run it** |
| the authority (the node or federation) | **the network** (reading) · **the station** (the station organ) |
| sealed *(as a fatal state)* | **shut** · **refused** — see the collision note below |
| local / unsettled transcript | **a run stays in this browser** |
| authored roster | **the crew list the curator signed** |

### One word that had two opposite jobs

`MISSION AUTHORITY SEALED` used **sealed** to mean *refused, nothing works*, while
a rack card's `sealed` state means *the curator has not opened this yet* — an
ordinary, healthy state. One word, two opposite readings, on one page. The fatal
banner now says **refused**; only a card the curator has not opened is **shut**.
The internal state name `sealed` is untouched.

### Two writing rules that came out of the sweep

- **Put the unit on the number.** `judged best 4` reads as a score of four. It is
  an action count and *lower is better* — the opposite reading. It is now
  `judged best 4 actions`.
- **Pluralize anything that can be one.** `1 charges left`, `1 actions`,
  `1 transitions`. Cheap to fix, and each one is a small proof that nobody read
  the sentence out loud.
- **Never renumber an emitted label.** Salvage's plates are `Expose plate 0`–
  `plate 5` in the descriptor. The browser must render them **raw**: a status line
  that said "plate 1" for the button labelled "plate 0" gives two people at one
  screen two names for one square. If a 0-based label should be 1-based, that is
  an *emitter* fix in Lean, never a browser rewrite.

### The gloss rule, stated once

An emitted tag rendered for a player gets **plain words AND the tag**, never the
words alone:

> The hidden answer — *drawn fresh for every run (`per-run-hidden-draw`) · never
> named on screen — the table answers, it does not show (`oracle-only`)*

A gloss that *replaced* its tag would be the page paraphrasing the wire, which is
how `oracle-only` quietly becomes whatever sounded close. A tag with no gloss in
`game-rack.js`'s tables falls through **verbatim**: that table teaches words for
tags it has been taught and says nothing about the rest.

## Where precision lives

Three layers, and they are not interchangeable:

1. **The card face and the tile** — plain language, no identifiers, no codes.
2. **The `<details>` "Verification" fold** — every digest, tag, revision and module
   path, glossed. A player who wants the working can open one element they have
   already learned once, and it is the same element on every card and every end
   screen.
3. **Source and tests** — the archaeology. Measurement dates, proxy headers,
   commit hashes, what a wall was and what landed. `CUSTODY_BLOCKERS[0].detail` is
   1,500 characters of exactly this and it is *pinned by tests*: it is a record,
   not a paragraph to render under a button.

The mistake this pass corrected most often was layer 3 leaking into layer 1.

## When you change one of these strings

Several tests assert exact text. Before you edit one, ask which kind it is:

- **A test that pinned a lie** should change with the copy. (Nothing in this pass
  found one; the strings were honest, only unreadable.)
- **A test that pinned a distinction must keep pinning it**, in the new words. The
  example to copy is `tests/game-rack.test.mjs`, "a card states only what the
  terminal has established about the catalog": it forbids curator-decision
  vocabulary on the `checking` and `refused` cards — and now also asserts that a
  genuinely `sealed` card **does** carry it. A prohibition with no positive control
  passes just as well when the vocabulary has been deleted entirely.

That last pattern is the one to reach for. `assert.doesNotMatch` on its own is a
guard that cannot go red the day the copy stops saying anything at all.

## Onboarding: the verdict, and what it is still missing

**Judged 2026-08-09: a first-time player could not learn what this is.** The
landing view opened on a status board of four verification tiles and a judged-session
panel; the word "drill" first appeared in the third panel of the overview grid, and
no sentence anywhere said *these are small puzzle games and you can play one now.*

One line was added to the overview heading — the fiction kept, a plain fact
appended:

> The ship moves by command. We go below to learn what is moving with us — a rack
> of short puzzle drills you can play right now, in this browser. A run stays here
> unless you deliberately send it.

That is the floor, not the ceiling. **Still owed**, and out of scope for a copy
pass because each needs layout or DOM work:

- The rack teaser is the *third* card in `.overview-grid`. The first thing a player
  sees below the status board should be the thing they can do.
- No drill can be started from the landing view. Every path to play is a nav click
  away.
- The four status tiles answer questions a first-time player has not asked yet.
  They are excellent for a returning player and they are the whole opening
  statement for a new one.
